# Bylaw Amendments

This page covers what has to happen when GSS changes its Bylaws or
Constitution, from the notice before the vote through to the filing and
notification afterwards.

The President owns this process. Under Bylaw 8.5.2 the President is
responsible for:

- the Constitution, Bylaws and Policy Manual (8.5.2(g))
- communication with members, including the website and email notices
  (8.5.2(b))
- carrying out Council decisions (8.5.2(c)(i))

{: .warning }
Passing a special resolution does **not** change the bylaws by itself. Under
the BC *Societies Act* s.17(3), a bylaw change "takes effect when the bylaw
alteration application is filed with the registrar." Until it is filed, the
old bylaws still apply. Bylaw 2.1.2 also requires Council to inform the
Registrar "immediately", so file within days of the vote, not weeks.

---

## Before the Meeting

| Requirement | Rule | Source |
|-------------|------|--------|
| Vote needed | Special resolution: two-thirds of votes cast, excluding blanks and abstentions | Bylaw 1 (definitions), Bylaw 2.1.1; *Societies Act* s.1 |
| Notice period | At least 14 days before a General Meeting, posted on the website **and** emailed to members. The Act also caps notice at 60 days before the meeting | Bylaw 5.3.1; *Societies Act* s.77 |
| Notice content | Date, time, place and proposed agenda, **plus the full text of every special resolution** to be voted on | Bylaw 5.3.2; *Societies Act* s.78(1) |

Bylaw amendments can also pass by referendum. A referendum needs the same
14-day notice on the website and by email (Bylaw 6.2).

## After the Vote: Checklist

| # | Step | Who | Source |
|---|------|-----|--------|
| 1 | Make sure the meeting minutes record the exact text of each special resolution and the vote result | President (or minute-taker) | *Societies Act* s.20(1)(i)–(j) |
| 2 | Prepare a **complete, consolidated** copy of the bylaws as amended (not only the changed sections), with the new revision date on the cover. Write a one-line description of the changes, e.g. "Changed bylaws 2.1, 5.4 and 12.1" | President, with CPC | BC Registries filing requirements |
| 3 | File a **bylaw alteration application** in Societies Online. You need the society's registry key, the date the special resolution passed, the change description, and the full bylaws as PDF or DOCX. The fee is $50 | President or GM | *Societies Act* s.17(1)–(3); Societies Regulation Sched. 2 |
| 4 | Download the **certified copy** of the amended bylaws that the Registrar issues. Save it to the `GSS Policy Manuals` folder in Dropbox as `GSSBylaws_YYYY-MM-COMPLETE.pdf`, and move the superseded version to an archive subfolder | President or GM | *Societies Act* s.17(4), s.20(1)(b); Bylaw 10.1 |
| 5 | Post the updated bylaws on the GSS website and announce the change to members, for example in the GSS Weekly newsletter | President | Bylaw 8.5.2(b) |
| 6 | Report the filing, and the date the changes took effect, to Council at its next meeting. Tell the executives and staff whose procedures changed | President | Bylaw 8.5.2(c)(i) |
| 7 | Update policies, guide pages and templates that cite the changed bylaw numbers. This includes the FO guide's bylaw references | President, with the affected executives | Bylaw 8.5.2(g) |

{: .note }
Needs confirmation: who holds the Societies Online registry key (GM or
President). Do not record the key in this guide. Step 5 is GSS practice under
Bylaw 8.5.2(b). The *Societies Act* does not require the bylaws to be posted
online, but it does give members access to them (see below).

## Member Requests for Copies

- Any member may inspect the bylaws, minutes and resolutions **free of
  charge** (*Societies Act* s.24(1); Bylaws 3.10.5 and 10.2).
- On request, a member is entitled to **one free copy** of the current
  constitution and bylaws (*Societies Act* s.27(4)(b)). It must be provided
  within **14 days** (s.27(1)–(2)).

Keeping the current bylaws on the website satisfies most requests
immediately.

## Typographical Corrections

Fixing spelling or grammar without changing meaning is **not** an amendment
and does not need a special resolution or a filing (Bylaw 2.2):

1. The Code and Policy Committee (CPC) must agree **unanimously** that the
   correction does not change the meaning (2.2.2).
2. CPC notifies Council at its next meeting. If any Councillor objects,
   Council must approve the correction by special resolution, or it is
   reverted (2.2.3).
3. Council notifies the membership of the corrections at the next AGM. If
   any member objects, the correction needs a special resolution or is
   reverted (2.2.4).

Stylistic changes can be made by CPC with the President's approval (2.2.5).

---

## Constitution Changes

Changes to the society's name or purposes follow the same route under
*Societies Act* s.15: a special resolution, then a constitution alteration
application in Societies Online ($50). The change takes effect only once it
is filed. A name change also needs a name reservation first.

---

## Policy Manual Amendments

Policy Manual changes are made by Council, not by the Registrar. They carry
their own requirements:

- Notice of the proposed change, with a brief rationale, must be in the
  agenda for the meeting where it is proposed (Bylaw 13.1.4).
- The change is made by ordinary resolution of the members or by special
  resolution of Council (Bylaw 13.1.5).
- An updated Policy Manual must be available at the Centre and on the
  website (Bylaw 13.2.1).
- The President ensures all Councillors receive notice of the changes
  (Bylaw 8.5.2(g)(ii)).
